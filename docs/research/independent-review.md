# WHITE ROOM independent research review

**19 November 2026 · Scientific and technical review**

**Decision.** Validate one learning workflow before building the desktop environment. The strongest rationale is for effortful retrieval with corrective feedback and spaced relearning. A curated module with an unseen delayed test can establish whether learners can use that workflow and whether it warrants a larger study. The proposed pilot would be difficult to interpret, and the proposed MVP adds several expensive systems that contribute little to that decision.

This review covers all six research documents. It checks selected high-impact claims against original articles, published syntheses, and upstream documentation. Platform findings come from documentation and source inspection via the cited projects. Runtime compatibility and product efficacy remain untested.

## Critical findings

### C1. The delayed tests change the outcome they are meant to measure

The roadmap compares one topic per condition, with tests at days 7 and 30. The engine adds a day-1 check and regular reviews. Retrieval on day 1 or 7 can improve day-30 performance even when the later questions are reworded. A day-30 score after those exposures can measure the effect of the whole regimen, provided exposures are intentional and recorded. It cannot by itself establish 30-day retention without interim testing. A single topic pair per person also leaves results vulnerable to prior knowledge, differences in item difficulty, cross-topic spillover, and material-specific effects. Multiple questions from one topic are correlated observations. Counting them as independent would overstate precision. The proposed rule of a ≥10 percentage-point lift for most of roughly 4–11 people can change with one item on a short test or one participant dropping out. [Roadmap, pilot design](implementation-roadmap.md#6-как-проверяем-гипотезу), [engine, delayed checks](cognitive-engine.md#6-отложенная-проверка-главная-метрика), [Roediger and Karpicke, randomized retrieval experiments](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x).

**Action.** Specify a comparison and a primary endpoint before enrollment. Use reviewed, held-out items from multiple matched objective sets. Randomize assignments within learner and counterbalance order across learners. Reserve distinct, comparable objectives for their first assessment at day 7 and day 30 if both horizons are meant to measure retention without earlier assessment exposure. Log scheduled and actual lag, all practice and assessment exposures, time spent including follow-up reviews, outside study, hints, and missing tests. Report the primary effect in percentage points with an uncertainty interval that respects assignment and clustering. Treat the proposed sample as a feasibility pilot. See the study design below.

### C2. Running learner or generated code creates a security boundary with no defined contract

The roadmap makes a runner part of the first GUI. The technical plan mentions a subprocess, `bwrap` or Firejail, no network, and time and memory limits. This leaves unanswered which files, credentials, inherited descriptors, desktop sockets and child processes the runner can reach. An ordinary subprocess inherits the user's access. Bubblewrap explicitly delegates the security model to its caller. Test success also covers only the behavior the tests encode. A failed or unavailable sandbox on a supported installation cannot fall back to a normal subprocess. [Technical feasibility, integrations and security](technical-feasibility.md#7-интеграции), [roadmap, build phases](implementation-roadmap.md#3-фазы), [Bubblewrap security model](https://github.com/containers/bubblewrap/blob/main/SECURITY.md).

**Action.** Use code reading, tracing, and short written or structured answers in the first programming module. If execution later proves necessary, define its isolation boundary and supported packaging first. Require explicit execution, a minimal filesystem and environment, resource limits for the entire process tree, and verification of isolation and cleanup before accepting arbitrary code. Keep authored tests and grading rubrics separate from learner submissions and generated content.

## High findings

### H1. The offline and local-first promises disagree with the data flows

The product permits cloud generation only and promises offline learning. The feasibility plan also sends learner answers for grading and hints, queues offline work, and previews data before the first send. A previously approved document does not authorize transmission of later answers, attachments or proprietary code. Window titles can expose documents and messages, domains reveal activity, and IDE errors can contain paths, source and credentials. An optional screenshot feature greatly enlarges the collection surface. A `0600` socket and self-declared client capabilities do not distinguish integrations running under the same user identity. Tauri capability configuration also warrants an audit of custom commands and their argument handling. [Product specification, privacy](product-specification.md#приватность-и-контроль), [technical feasibility, cloud and IPC](technical-feasibility.md#6-ии-облако-и-локальная-модель), [Tauri capabilities](https://v2.tauri.app/security/capabilities/).

**Action.** For the pilot, store only curated content, answers or scores needed for evaluation, and minimal activity within the learning workflow. Keep grading local. Use a data inventory with purpose, granularity, retention, deletion and export behavior before introducing any integration. If cloud authoring is validated later, approve the exact payload for each deliberate submission, exclude automatic replay and learner-answer upload, disclose provider retention and deletion limits, and handle absent Secret Service without silently storing credentials in plaintext. Treat imported material as untrusted data and keep generation separate from shell commands, files beyond the selection, and publishing or grading actions.

### H2. The Linux compatibility table conflates window discovery, active focus, and control

Hyprland's event interface is documented. `activewindow` includes class and title, while `activewindowv2` supplies a window address. Neither event alone establishes initial state, reconciles a reconnect, or defines a stable identity across restarts. Sway's own IPC has a window event and a tree with focus information. That API is specific to Sway and compatible implementations. The optional `wlr-foreign-toplevel-management` protocol has activation state and control requests. The separate staging `ext-foreign-toplevel-list` protocol gives identifiers, titles and app IDs without a focused-window signal or close operation. Compatibility depends on the compositor, advertised protocol and version. KWin scripting and a GNOME Shell extension are separate integrations with their own version and installation constraints. X11's `_NET_ACTIVE_WINDOW` assumes an EWMH-capable window manager. XWayland is not a route to observing native Wayland windows. The roadmap specifies Hyprland only, the product and feasibility documents also require X11, and Firefox appears as both an MVP integration and an open decision. [Technical feasibility, platform matrix](technical-feasibility.md#1-главное-ограничение-на-wayland-нет-общего-api), [roadmap, scope](implementation-roadmap.md#2-объём-mvp), [Hyprland IPC](https://wiki.hypr.land/IPC/), [Sway IPC](https://man.archlinux.org/man/sway-ipc.7.en), [wlroots protocol](https://github.com/swaywm/wlr-protocols/blob/master/unstable/wlr-foreign-toplevel-management-unstable-v1.xml), [Wayland staging protocol](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/wayland/wayland-protocols/-/blob/main/staging/ext-foreign-toplevel-list/ext-foreign-toplevel-list-v1.xml), [KWin scripting API](https://develop.kde.org/docs/plasma/kwin/api/), [GNOME extension listing](https://extensions.gnome.org/extension/5592/focused-window-d-bus/), [EWMH specification](https://specifications.freedesktop.org/wm/latest-single/).

**Action.** Make the learning flow independent of any compositor. If tracking later earns a place, publish a versioned capability matrix with explicit unavailable states. For a first Hyprland adapter, verify initial snapshot, event reconciliation, compositor restart, missing focus, browser versus native windows, and lock, suspend and resume. Drop titles at ingestion and avoid drawing conclusions about the task from a window class. Treat dispatcher actions such as moving or closing a window as shell interactions that can interrupt work or lose state.

### H3. Browser blocking and emergency release have independent failure modes

The proposed emergency guarantee assumes a global shortcut, a usable tray and a live browser bridge. Tauri's upstream Linux global-hotkey support is X11-oriented. Wayland shortcut portals depend on the compositor backend and user interaction. An AppImage running through XWayland does not give it a dependable desktop-wide shortcut. Chrome MV3 dynamic rules survive browser restart and extension updates. Session rules are cleared on browser shutdown or extension update, but do not by themselves expire when a desktop process crashes. The MV3 service worker has its own lifecycle. Native messaging requires a discoverable host for the actual browser package and extension ID. Rules apply within the configured browser and profile. Alternate profiles, browsers and already loaded content limit coverage. [Technical feasibility, blocking](technical-feasibility.md#2-блокировка), [Tauri upstream shortcut issue](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/3578), [GlobalShortcuts portal](https://flatpak.github.io/xdg-desktop-portal/docs/doc-org.freedesktop.portal.GlobalShortcuts.html), [Chrome rule lifetimes](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/declarativeNetRequest/), [Chrome native messaging](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/native-messaging), [Chrome service worker lifecycle](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/service-workers/lifecycle/), [Mozilla confined native messaging design](https://firefox-source-docs.mozilla.org/toolkit/components/extensions/webextensions/native-messaging-portal-design.html).

**Action.** Defer blocking from the learning pilot. If tested separately, start with voluntary, short-lived top-level site blocking in one supported Chromium package. The extension should own release, permit immediate disengagement in its own UI, and reconcile expired rules after desktop exit, disconnect, worker restart, suspend or update. Verify unattended recovery and recovery when the UI, tray or shortcut is unavailable. Scope the roadmap's latency target to tested paths and versions. Use a compositor binding or a tested portal as an additional convenience. Drop process termination, privileged DNS and workstation-wide enforcement from the design under test.

### H4. FSRS evidence and input semantics are overstated

Spacing research supports distributed practice. The FSRS benchmark measures how closely predicted probabilities fit historical recall labels, conditional on the data and evaluation setup. That result does not establish that FSRS yields higher retention for the same time, or that a flashcard model transfers to long problems, hints, and IDE-assisted answers. The quoted log-loss range has no pinned benchmark version, split, aggregation, comparator implementation or outcome definition. Anki's `Hard` means successful recall with difficulty. Automatically treating a hint-assisted answer as successful, or speed as evidence for `Easy`, changes the label and can lengthen intervals on fragile material. The proposed 1,000-review threshold is a heuristic rather than a prerequisite established by the research. [Scientific foundations, spacing](scientific-foundations.md#22-интервальные-повторения-и-fsrs-уровень-a), [engine, FSRS mapping](cognitive-engine.md#31-память-fsrs-для-каждого-item-r), [Cepeda et al., spacing synthesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/), [Anki deck options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html#fsrs), [benchmark maintained by the project](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark).

**Action.** Pilot a transparent fixed schedule with due reviews and corrective feedback. If flashcards become a core workflow, use a pinned FSRS version and defaults on eligible unaided card reviews. Keep correctness, assistance, latency and self-rating as separate observations. Record an unaided miss as a miss before grading any repair attempt. Evaluate scheduling changes prospectively on delayed recall and workload, and evaluate any personalized fit on held-out later reviews.

### H5. The skill score and fatigue rules turn weak proxies into decisions

A weighted Beta update can summarize performance on a specified task pool. Its mean is not an identified probability of success on arbitrary new tasks when difficulty varies, tasks share templates, and the planner selects tasks based on past scores. Shrinking towards the prior is an unvalidated time-decay heuristic and can raise the estimate for a learner below the prior. A single per-skill value cannot select tasks with a target 60–80% success probability without task difficulty information. The 0.7 prerequisite gate has no calibration evidence. Similarly, slower responses or lower accuracy in a later block can reflect harder items, use of reference material, or interruptions. The rule that ends a session and tells the learner they are fatigued conflicts with the engine's later advisory-only rule and the product's skippable breaks. Microbreak results support modest changes in reported vigor and fatigue and found no statistically significant overall performance effect. They do not establish 25–50 minute blocks, a 90-minute trigger, a three-hour ceiling for learning, or accuracy-driven stopping. [Engine, skill model and session rules](cognitive-engine.md#3-три-уровня-модели), [product, session behavior](product-specification.md#32-во-время-сессии), [Albulescu et al., microbreak synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272460).

**Action.** Report counts and denominators for first unaided attempts on reviewed tasks, with the assessed objectives, difficulty bands where available, and coverage. Describe unseen parallel tasks as evidence of near transfer under specified conditions. Record assisted or repeated attempts as practice. Offer user-controlled breaks and stopping, with an optional neutral check-in. Keep confidence prompts optional and sparse. A small forecast error can be sampling noise or biased item difficulty. Require repeated, comparable observations before concluding that anybody overestimates performance, and leave scheduling unchanged in the pilot.

### H6. Item quality is an assessment problem as well as an authoring problem

The proposed 30-second review of ten generated items could catch obvious wording defects. It offers little assurance that references support the answer, distractors target plausible misconceptions, variants have similar difficulty, or the same template appears only on one side of the practice–assessment boundary. User-authored and imported items also vary in quality. A CAS needs an explicit mathematical domain and equivalence policy. Unit tests establish agreement on sampled cases, and exact text matching can reject valid alternatives. The engine describes LLM feedback as qualitative while assigning it weight in the skill model. Flagging a bad item after practice also requires a defined way to exclude its affected scores and derived state. [Engine, content and quality](cognitive-engine.md#9-генерация-контента-и-контроль-качества), [product, domain checks](product-specification.md#4-домены), [Doughty et al., programming MCQs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3636243.3636256).

**Action.** Begin with human-authored, versioned items tied to objectives, with accepted alternatives and explicit rubrics. Have a reviewer independently solve and score a sample of each form before enrollment. Freeze the assessment bank and scoring rules. Preserve item versions and invalidation reasons so aggregate scores can be recalculated without quietly changing historical results. If AI authoring is later compared with human authoring, assess factual accuracy, answer-key accuracy, content coverage, review time and agreement against independent review. Use commentary from a model only as optional practice feedback.

### M1. Local storage needs a testable deletion and recovery policy

SQLite WAL mode introduces sidecar files. Ordinary deletion, file mode `0600`, and disk encryption address different threats. Disk encryption prevalence is unsupported, and full disk encryption principally protects data at rest while the volume is locked. Indefinite aggregates, exports, logs and backups also complicate the promise of full deletion. A portable core facilitates testing, but the design puts compositor adapters, protocol negotiation and the learning scheduler in the same proposed crate. The advertised RAM budget also needs to include the webview and helpers to be comparable. [Technical feasibility, stack and storage](technical-feasibility.md#4-варианты-стека), [SQLite WAL](https://www.sqlite.org/wal.html), [SQLite secure deletion](https://www.sqlite.org/pragma.html#pragma_secure_delete), [Tauri Linux graphics and AppImage notes](https://v2.tauri.app/develop/debug/linux-graphics/).

**Action.** For a later app, prefer one local owner for the database, small transactional attempt records, versioned content and scoring, and a documented way to recover an interrupted save. WAL should follow an actual concurrency requirement. Define deletion across app-managed files, logs and stores. Explain that separately copied exports, provider data and OS backups have their own lifecycles. Defer interprocess integrations. Decide on the first GUI stack from keyboard access, fonts, packaging and measured behavior on the supported distro and compositor. If comparing Tauri with Qt/QML, use the same workflow, idle and active measurements, and whole-process-tree proportional memory. Treat documented graphics workarounds as test cases.

## Learning mechanisms to promote into the first workflow

1. **Successive relearning.** The documents mention retrieval and spacing but omit an explicit repair and relearning criterion. After an unaided miss, show corrective feedback, let the learner try again after intervening practice, and schedule another successful retrieval on a separate day. Cap retries to avoid frustration. Count the repair as practice, while preserving the first-attempt outcome. Evidence is strongest for recall of course concepts and definitions. Use parallel problems to test whether the procedure generalizes to skill. [Karpicke and Roediger, repeated retrieval](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408), [Rawson, Dunlosky and Sciartelli, course learning experiments](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1036741), [Rawson and Dunlosky, relearning experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23088488/).
2. **Specific corrective feedback followed by a new attempt.** The plan often stops at revealing the answer. Explain the error, give a cue or short worked step, and follow it with a comparable problem that requires the learner to use the correction. Use a rubric where several answers are valid. Feedback effects vary by what information the feedback supplies. [Wisniewski et al., feedback synthesis](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087), [Butler and Roediger, corrective feedback](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18491500/).
3. **Worked examples with fading and selective mixing.** The science document already names both ideas. For novices, start with a worked example, proceed to a completion exercise, then give a comparable independent problem. Once learners can execute individual methods, mix easily confused types and ask them to choose a method. Keep the mix small and specify a delayed transfer item. Evidence for interleaving is stronger in some category learning and mathematics settings than in arbitrary collections of tasks. [Renkl, Atkinson and Große, fading experiments](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:TRUC.0000021815.74806.f6), [Rohrer et al., randomized classroom trial](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000367), [Brunmair and Richter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/).

## Recommended MVP and validation

### Smallest testable product

Use a single introductory programming module, such as tracing loops and predicting their outputs, with short authored explanations and an answer key. The learner opens a module, sees a worked example when appropriate, completes unaided retrieval or a worked-step completion, gets corrective feedback, and returns on scheduled days for another retrieval attempt. Keep an unseen parallel test available at the specified delay. Initial implementation can be a worksheet with scheduled follow-ups or a minimal local interface. An app becomes justified when the study shows friction the manual workflow cannot resolve. Store an objective and item version with each attempt, along with its role, exposure time, score and assistance. A simple due list, a score with its denominator and a local export cover the pilot. Provide a keyboard-accessible flow.

### Pilot design and decision gates

1. **Dry run with curated content.** Measure authoring and review time per objective, how often the rubric rejects reasonable answers, whether people can complete a session independently, and whether the due review and delayed test are easy to find. Freeze revised forms before the comparative pilot.
2. **Choose one primary question.** Compare the full learning workflow with equal-time restudy or a defined usual-study workflow using the same material and comparable contact times. This estimates the effect of the whole regimen. For a later incremental comparison, hold retrieval, feedback and total exposure constant and compare against a simple due list or Anki workflow. This tests whether WHITE ROOM adds value beyond the established mechanisms. If usual study already includes retrieval, record that rather than treating it as restudy.
3. **Assign and assess.** Use several matched objective sets per person, randomize their assignment to condition, and counterbalance order across participants. Use a short baseline on separate items to check prior knowledge. Use day-7 first-exposure unaided retention as the pilot's primary outcome. Keep day-30 first-exposure retention and new-task success as exploratory outcomes on predefined, comparable material. Give both arms identical test conditions. Document and count interim reviews as part of the intervention, and keep held-out prompts out of authoring, practice and feedback. Where grading requires judgment, conceal assignment and check agreement on a sample.
4. **Analyze at the assignment level.** Predefine the date windows, missed-test handling, accepted answers and the minimum effect that would justify a larger study. Report denominators, adherence, actual exposure and lag, group and participant-level summaries, missingness by arm, and paired effects with uncertainty. Sensitivity-check differential dropout and outside study. Use parallel forms with reviewed difficulty and avoid pooling recall and transfer into a single score. A small pilot may establish that the protocol works, with wide uncertainty around the estimated benefit.
5. **Decide what to fund.** Before enrolling, set feasible thresholds for independent completion, follow-up completion, scoring consistency and authoring burden. Continue to a larger study when the protocol meets those thresholds and the estimates leave a practically meaningful benefit plausible. Revise the materials or workflow when failure is observable there. Size the larger study for the minimum useful lift, observed variability and clustering, with allowance for attrition. Evaluate distraction controls separately against their stated outcomes and require a concrete learning or workflow benefit before expanding the platform integrations.
