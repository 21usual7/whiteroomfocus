# WHITE ROOM — Research-First Development

You are a senior software architect, cognitive science researcher, AI engineer, and product designer.

Your task is to research, design, and eventually build **WHITE ROOM** — an advanced desktop application for deep concentration, accelerated learning, long-term memory retention, and measurable cognitive development.

The project takes aesthetic inspiration from the White Room in *Classroom of the Elite*, but its actual mechanisms must be grounded in scientific evidence rather than fictional concepts.

**IMPORTANT: DO NOT START IMPLEMENTING THE APPLICATION YET.**

Your first task is to conduct comprehensive research, challenge my assumptions, discover better approaches, and produce a technically realistic product specification.

Do not simply agree with my ideas. Identify what works, what doesn't, and what could work better.

## 1. Product Vision

WHITE ROOM should be a personalized cognitive training environment, not another Pomodoro timer or generic productivity app.

The user enters Focus Mode and selects a domain:

- Mathematics
- Research
- German
- Cybersecurity
- Programming
- Custom domain

Each domain has specialized learning modes, tools, objectives, and evaluation methods.

For example:

Mathematics → Learning / Problem Solving / Recall.

Cybersecurity → Research / Labs / Reverse Engineering / Practice.

Programming → Learning / Implementation / Debugging / Problem Solving.

Before a session, an intelligent onboarding system asks a few contextual questions to understand the user's goals, current knowledge, task requirements, and necessary applications.

It then generates a personalized training protocol.

Sessions may span up to four hours, divided into scientifically justified focus blocks, recovery periods, and knowledge assessments.

## 2. Research Areas

Investigate the following topics using peer-reviewed studies, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, reputable academic publications, and relevant technical documentation.

### A. Cognitive Science and Learning

Research:

- Active Recall and Retrieval Practice
- Spaced Repetition and FSRS
- Interleaving
- Deliberate Practice
- Cognitive Load Theory
- Metacognition
- Desirable Difficulties
- Deep Work and sustained attention
- Memory consolidation
- Feedback timing
- Learning transfer
- Self-explanation and elaboration
- Effects of breaks, fatigue, and sleep on learning

Determine which techniques have strong empirical support, which are context-dependent, and which are controversial.

Distinguish actual improvements in learning from improvements in perceived productivity.

### B. Focus and Environmental Control

Investigate:

- Application and website blocking
- Distraction detection
- Task switching and attention fragmentation
- Background noise, white noise, brown noise, music, and silence
- Whether controlled environmental distractions can improve distraction resilience
- Adaptive session lengths
- Recovery intervals
- Ethical and safe attention-training methods

Do not assume that unpleasant sounds, extreme isolation, or prolonged sessions improve cognition.

### C. AI Cognitive Monitoring

Research how a privacy-first AI agent could observe task-relevant computer activity and estimate:

- Task alignment
- Context switching
- Focus interruptions
- Task progression
- Signs that the user may need a break
- Learning outcomes

Investigate active-window tracking, application metadata, optional screen analysis, IDE integrations, browser extensions, and local AI models.

Critically evaluate what these signals can and cannot establish.

Never equate keyboard activity or time spent in an application with actual learning.

The system must be transparent, opt-in, locally controlled, and capable of functioning without continuous screen recording.

### D. Personalized Cognitive Model

Explore how WHITE ROOM could gradually construct a personalized learning profile based on:

- Knowledge retention
- Recall accuracy
- Problem-solving performance
- Error patterns
- Task difficulty
- Learning speed
- Transfer of knowledge
- Long-term progress

Investigate adaptive learning algorithms, knowledge tracing, Bayesian models, FSRS, and relevant alternatives.

Avoid scientifically unsupported claims about IQ improvement, intelligence enhancement, or cognitive degradation.

### E. Existing Products

Analyze relevant features, strengths, weaknesses, and opportunities in products such as:

- Anki
- SuperMemo
- Focusmate
- Freedom
- Cold Turkey
- RescueTime
- Rize
- Brain.fm
- Brilliant
- Duolingo

Identify opportunities for meaningful differentiation rather than simply combining existing features.

## 3. Technical Feasibility

The primary target platform is **Linux desktop**.

Research the feasibility of:

- Tauri + React + TypeScript
- Rust-based system integration
- SQLite
- Claude API and alternative AI providers
- Local inference
- Browser extensions
- IDE integrations
- Active-window monitoring
- Application blocking
- Wayland vs X11 compatibility
- Secure permissions and privacy
- Offline functionality
- Resource consumption

Compare reasonable alternatives before selecting a stack.

Do not assume that system-wide monitoring or application blocking is universally supported.

## 4. Product Innovation

Go beyond my initial feature ideas.

Propose original mechanisms that could make WHITE ROOM meaningfully better than conventional focus applications.

Prioritize features that improve measurable learning outcomes.

Consider:

- Adaptive learning protocols
- Knowledge decay prediction
- AI-generated recall challenges
- Domain-specific training environments
- Personalized difficulty adjustment
- Long-term retention analytics
- Intelligent session planning
- Progress-based feedback

Clearly distinguish research-backed mechanisms from experimental hypotheses.

## 5. Research Deliverables

Create the following Markdown files inside a `docs/research/` directory:

1. `scientific-foundations.md` — Evidence review, study references, limitations, and practical implications.
2. `competitive-analysis.md` — Existing products and differentiation opportunities.
3. `technical-feasibility.md` — Platform constraints, integration options, and architecture tradeoffs.
4. `product-specification.md` — Detailed user experience, features, domain-specific workflows, and functional requirements.
5. `cognitive-engine.md` — Proposed adaptive learning and memory architecture.
6. `implementation-roadmap.md` — MVP scope, development phases, dependencies, risks, and validation milestones.

For important scientific claims, include source links, publication dates, study types, and evidence strength.

Use actual source research where browsing is available. Never fabricate citations. If external research is unavailable, explicitly identify what remains unverified.

## 6. Development Process

Follow this sequence:

**Phase 1 — Research:** Investigate the scientific and technical foundations.

**Phase 2 — Critical Evaluation:** Challenge the initial concept, identify weaknesses, and propose improvements.

**Phase 3 — Product Architecture:** Define components, data flows, security boundaries, and integration contracts.

**Phase 4 — MVP Planning:** Determine the smallest product capable of testing whether WHITE ROOM genuinely improves learning outcomes.

**Phase 5 — Implementation:** Begin only after I review and approve the research and architecture.

Before implementation, present the most important findings, architectural decisions, unresolved questions, and your recommended direction.

Do not create application source code during the research phase.

## 7. Quality Standards

- Think like a researcher, not a marketing copywriter.
- Prioritize evidence over intuition.
- Prefer measurable outcomes over arbitrary productivity scores.
- Separate established findings from hypotheses.
- Avoid unnecessary complexity.
- Design for privacy and user control.
- Preserve breaks, accessibility, and emergency exit controls.
- Do not optimize for compulsive usage or maximum screen time.
- Challenge assumptions, including my own.
- Be ambitious about innovation but realistic about implementation.

**Begin with research, not coding.**
